A VR headset is a device worn on your head like a pair of goggles that completely covers your eyes to place you inside a three-dimensional computer-generated world by showing you tiny screens placed inches from your eyes through special lenses that focus the images so they look life-sized, and these games work because motion tracking measures how your head tilts and turns while image shifting redraws the virtual world on the screens the moment you move alongside hand controllers that track your gestures so you can pick up objects, plus stereoscopic 3D sends slightly different pictures to your left and right eyes to create depth while spatial audio shifts sound effects based on which way you turn your head, and companies try to stop people from modding these systems by using locked bootloaders that block custom operating systems, file encryption that scrambles game data so players cannot rewrite code, automatic updates that patch security flaws to disable known community tools, server-side checks that force the headset to verify its official software before allowing online play, and hardware security chips that require every piece of software to carry a digital signature from the manufacturer before it can boot up at all.
